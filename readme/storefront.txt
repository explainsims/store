Storefront 1.1.2
Storefront puts all of Lennox's free Mac apps in one place. Install any of them in a click, and keep them all up
to date from the menu bar.


WHAT'S IN THIS FOLDER

  • Storefront.app: the app
  • Install Storefront.command: installs it (run this)
  • Read Me.txt: this file


BEFORE YOU START

  • Any Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel) running macOS 14 or later.


INSTALL

  1. Double-click "Install Storefront.command".

     If macOS says it can't be opened ("Apple could not verify…"): click
     Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click
     "Open Anyway", then double-click the installer again.

     Or open Terminal, type  bash  followed by a space, drag
     "Install Storefront.command" into the Terminal window, and press Return.

  2. The installer copies Storefront into Applications and opens it.

  Prefer to do it by hand? Drag Storefront into Applications and open it. The
  first time, macOS blocks it: go to System Settings → Privacy & Security
  and click "Open Anyway".


USING Storefront

  • Click the striped awning in the menu bar. A red dot on it means updates
    are ready: click Update, or Update All.

  • "Open Storefront…" shows every app. Click one to see what it does,
    screenshots and what's new, then Install. Apps you install here open
    straight away, with no "Open Anyway" step.

  • Storefront checks for updates when it starts, when your Mac wakes, and
    every six hours, and tells you when there are some. Turn that off in
    Settings › General.

  • Got an idea, or found something wrong? Every app's page has "Send
    feedback" at the bottom. It's private and anonymous: only the maker of
    these apps reads it, and your name and sign-in aren't sent. Send as
    many as you like.

  • SSIS staff: Settings › Account › Sign In with SSIS shows the apps that
    only work at school, on their own SSIS page.


GOOD TO KNOW

  • Storefront updates itself: Storefront Settings › General › Check for
    Storefront Updates.

  • To uninstall: quit Storefront from its menu and drag it from Applications
    to the Trash. Apps it installed stay.

  • Storefront is free and open source. It isn't from the App Store and isn't
    notarized by Apple, which is why macOS asks you to confirm it the
    first time.
