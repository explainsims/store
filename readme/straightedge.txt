Straightedge 1.2.2
Turn lab data in Google Sheets into a proper physics graph. Select your
data, click Graph, and Straightedge draws it beside the sheet with error bars,
a best-fit line and the steepest and shallowest lines, and gives the
gradient and intercept with their uncertainties and units.


WHAT'S IN THIS FOLDER

  • Straightedge.app: the app
  • Install Straightedge.command: installs it (run this)
  • Read Me.txt: this file


BEFORE YOU START

  • Any Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel) running macOS 14 or later. It was made and tested
    on macOS 27.
  • Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge (or both).


INSTALL

  1. Double-click "Install Straightedge.command".

     If macOS says it can't be opened ("Apple could not verify…"): click
     Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click
     "Open Anyway", then double-click the installer again.

     Or open Terminal, type  bash  followed by a space, drag
     "Install Straightedge.command" into the Terminal window, and press Return.

  2. The installer moves Straightedge into Applications and opens it.

  3. Add Straightedge's extension to Chrome (and Edge, if you use it). It's free
     and doesn't need the Web Store:
       • Open chrome://extensions (edge://extensions in Edge).
       • Turn on Developer mode.
       • Click Load unpacked and choose the folder Straightedge shows you:
         ~/Library/Application Support/Straightedge/Extension
         Quickest way: click Copy Path in Straightedge's window. In the
         window Load unpacked opens, press ⌘⇧G (Command-Shift-G),
         then ⌘V to paste, then Return, and click Select.
     A welcome page opens to show it worked.

  4. Pin Straightedge to the toolbar: click the puzzle piece at the top right
     of the browser, then the pin next to Straightedge.


USING Straightedge

  • In a Google Sheet, select your table: the headers and the numbers. Put
    each uncertainty column beside its quantity, for example
    "Length L / m", "ΔL / m", "Period T / s", "ΔT / s". A fixed uncertainty
    can go in the header instead: "Length L / m ± 0.001".
    Repeated trials ("T1", "T2", "T3") are averaged for you.

  • Click Graph, next to Share (or press ⌥⇧G, or click Straightedge in the
    toolbar). The graph opens in a panel beside the sheet. The panel
    belongs to that sheet: it hides when you go to another tab and comes
    back when you return. (In any other tab, the toolbar button opens a
    large page where you can paste a table to graph.)

  • Open large in a tab (the button at the top) shows the graph big, for
    the board or a projector. "Hide the controls" there leaves just the
    graph and the results. The panel and the large tab stay in step: change
    a title, a transform or a point in one and the other follows.

  • Choose what goes up the side (y) and along the bottom (x), and their
    uncertainties, with the menus at the top.

  • Click the title, an axis title or a line in the key to change its
    words: the version worked out for you (like "T² / s²"), one made from
    your column headings (like "Period squared, T² / s²"), or your own.

  • Linearise with one click: y², √x, 1/x, log–log or ln y, or pick any
    power or log from the menu beside each axis. The error bars and the
    axis titles follow. If the data curves, Straightedge suggests what to try.

  • The steepest and shallowest lines go through every error bar. Drag their
    ends to draw them by eye, the way the IB guide describes. If no straight
    line fits every bar, Straightedge says so and points to the odd point.
    Click any point to leave it out of the fit, and again to put it back.

  • "Work out g, k or another quantity from the gradient": if your theory
    says the gradient is, say, 4π²/g, type  g = 4π²/m  and get g with its
    uncertainty and unit. Use m for the gradient and c for the intercept.
    Add another measured value after a semicolon:
    k = 4π²M/m; M = 0.200 ± 0.001 kg

  • Copy image puts a sharp picture of the graph on the clipboard: paste it
    into Google Docs, Slides, Word or Pages. Put in sheet places it beside
    your data. The ••• menu downloads PNG or SVG, copies the results as
    text, and adds the processed columns (like T² and Δ(T²)) to the sheet
    as formulas.

  • The graph updates by itself when you change a number in the sheet.

  • Settings (the gear): IB or AP, how the gradient's uncertainty is worked
    out (max/min lines through every bar, through the first and last bars,
    or the least-squares standard error), rounding, the graph's look, and
    whether to suggest linearisations.


GOOD TO KNOW

  • Nothing leaves your computer. Straightedge reads only the cells you graph,
    in your browser, and works everything out on this Mac. It has no
    server, no account and no tracking.

  • It reads cells the way a copy would, without touching your clipboard,
    so it can't read a sheet whose owner has turned off copying.

  • To update: use Check for Updates… in Straightedge, or unzip a newer copy and
    run its installer. The extension updates itself from the same folder.

  • To uninstall: remove Straightedge from chrome://extensions, then drag
    Straightedge from Applications to the Trash.

  • Straightedge is free and open source. It isn't from the App Store and isn't
    notarized by Apple, which is why macOS asks you to confirm it the
    first time. It isn't affiliated with Google, Vernier or Desmos.
