Corkboard 1.2.1
A board for the front of the classroom. Put it on the TV or projector and pin
timers, a name picker, groups, a voice-level meter, a traffic light, work
symbols, polls, text, pictures, videos and more to it. A deck holds the
screens for a lesson.


WHAT'S IN THIS FOLDER

  • Corkboard.app: the app
  • Install Corkboard.command: installs it (run this)
  • Read Me.txt: this file


BEFORE YOU START

  • Any Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel) running macOS 14 or later. It was made and tested
    on macOS 27.
  • A TV or projector is optional. With one set to Extend (not Mirror),
    the board goes on the TV and a control window stays on your Mac.


INSTALL

  1. Double-click "Install Corkboard.command".

     If macOS says it can't be opened ("Apple could not verify…"): click
     Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click
     "Open Anyway", then double-click the installer again.

     Or open Terminal, type  bash  followed by a space, drag
     "Install Corkboard.command" into the Terminal window, and press Return.

  2. The installer moves Corkboard into Applications and opens it, with a
     first deck to try.

  Prefer to do it by hand? Drag Corkboard into Applications and open it. The
  first time, macOS blocks it: go to System Settings → Privacy & Security
  and click "Open Anyway".


USING Corkboard

  • The bar along the bottom adds widgets. Click one and it appears on
    the board. "All" shows every widget, and "Edit the Bar…" there picks
    which ones sit on the bar.

  • Each widget remembers how you set it up last time: a new timer
    starts at your last length and sounds, a text card in your last
    font, and so on (never the content: words, votes, answers). Turn it
    off, or forget them, in Settings → General.

  • Drag a widget to move it, and its corners to resize it. The round
    handle under it rotates it. Click a widget to see its toolbar:
    settings, show on every screen (a pin appears on it), spotlight,
    more (duplicate, lock, front and back), delete.

  • Background (on the bar) changes what's behind the widgets: gradients,
    moving backgrounds, colours, paper, the Mac's own wallpapers, your own
    pictures and slideshows.

  • The strip at the top right holds the deck's screens. + adds one; the
    arrow keys move between them. The deck's name at the top left opens
    your decks, templates and more.

  • Draw (on the bar, or the D key) lets you write over the board.

  • Present (top right) puts the board on a TV or projector. If the TV is
    set to Extend, presenter view opens on your Mac: a copy of the board,
    buttons for each widget, your notes for the screen, and what's next.

  • Keys: 1 blurs the screen ("Eyes on me"), 2 plays a chime, B hides the
    bar, F is full screen, K lists every shortcut.

  • The menu-bar icon has quick tools that work over any app, Keynote and
    videos included: a timer, pick a name, make groups, a traffic light,
    the voice-level meter and blur. Anywhere: Control-Option-T starts a
    timer, -N picks a name, -G makes groups, -B blurs the screen.

  • Class lists: Settings → Classes. Type or paste names, or import a
    spreadsheet's CSV. SSIS staff: in Rollbook's Settings → Privacy, turn
    on "Share my classes with Corkboard" and all your classes appear, any
    time. "This class" and "Next class" follow the timetable, with anyone
    away left out.

  • Exam board: Decks → New Deck → Exam board. Give it a title, the start
    time, the reading time and the length. Reading time and writing time
    start by themselves, with a big clock, the times and your rules.

  • Quiet scene: in the voice level's settings, choose Scene (or start
    from the Quiet animals template). While the room stays under the
    limit, animals come into a picture one by one. When it's too loud,
    the weather turns and they hurry away. Each class keeps a collection
    of every animal it has spotted (settings › Collection › Show…).

  • Hall pass (in All): click Sign out, then a name (and where to, if
    you like). The board shows who's out and for how long, amber when
    it's been a while. One at a time unless you turn that off. Today's
    trips are listed in its settings and presenter view, never on the
    board, and clear themselves the next day.

  • Lesson pie and Now and next: in a timetable's settings, Show as.
    The pie shows the lesson's parts in colours with a hand at now;
    Now and next shows what's on in big letters and what comes next.
    With Rollbook, "Start with Rollbook's bell" times it to your block.
    Or start from the Lesson plan template.

  • Escape rooms: Decks → New Deck → Escape room. Padlocks (in All) take
    a code of numbers, letters, arrows or colours; the right one opens
    to a message and, if you choose, the next screen. The answer is
    never shown on the board, only in settings and presenter view.
    Links can go to a screen of the deck, so a deck can branch.

  • Weather (in All): the temperature now, a thermometer (°C, °F and
    kelvin), the next hours, the week, or the air quality (AQI). Search
    for any city in its settings; it starts with your Mac's city. Data
    from Open-Meteo; only the city's position is sent.

  • Countdown: each event has a picture (a test, a trip, Tết…). Name it
    "Physics test" and it suggests one.

  • Seating plan (in All): rows, desks in pairs, group tables or a
    horseshoe. "Seat the class" fills it (shuffled, A to Z, or by the
    deck's groups) and keeps chosen pairs apart. Click two seats to swap.

  • Lesson autopilot (with Rollbook): in a deck's menu, "Opens for a
    Block". When that block starts, the deck opens by itself, with a
    button to go back to the one you had open.

  • Phone remote: Settings → Voting, or the menu-bar icon. Turn it on,
    scan the code with your phone and type the 6-digit PIN shown on your
    Mac (never on the board). Your phone then changes screens, starts
    timers, picks names, rolls dice and more. Same Wi-Fi as your Mac.

  • Polls students answer on their phones: in a poll's settings, set
    "How to vote" to students' phones. A QR code and an address appear on
    the board. Phones must be on the same Wi-Fi as your Mac. Some school
    networks keep devices apart; then use buttons on the board instead.


PERMISSIONS IT MAY ASK FOR

  • Microphone: only for the voice-level meter. Nothing is recorded.
  • Camera: only for the camera widget (a webcam, a document camera or
    your iPhone's camera). Nothing is recorded.
  • Incoming connections: only if your Mac's firewall is on, when students
    vote from their phones. Click Allow.


GOOD TO KNOW

  • The internet is only used for NASA's picture of the day, the weather,
    videos and web pages you add, and Check for Updates.

  • Your decks are saved as you go, in iCloud Drive → Corkboard, so your
    other Mac sees them too. Deleted decks wait 30 days in Recently
    Deleted (in the Decks window) before they go for good.

  • To share a deck with a colleague: the deck menu → Share Deck…. They
    double-click the file to open it in their own Corkboard.

  • To update: Corkboard → Check for Updates…, or unzip a newer copy and
    run its installer. Your decks and settings stay.

  • To uninstall: quit Corkboard (from its menu-bar icon or with ⌘Q) and
    drag it from Applications to the Trash. Your decks stay in iCloud Drive.

  • Corkboard is free and open source, an alternative to Classroomscreen.
    It isn't made by or affiliated with Classroomscreen. It isn't from the
    App Store and isn't notarized by Apple, which is why macOS asks you to
    confirm it the first time.
