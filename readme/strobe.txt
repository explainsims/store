Strobe 1.0.1
Video motion analysis for physics labs. Open a video of something moving,
set the scale and the axes, click the object on each frame (or let Strobe
track it), and get position, velocity and acceleration as a table and
graphs, with fits, uncertainties and a strobe picture for worksheets.


WHAT'S IN THIS FOLDER

  • Strobe.app: the app
  • Install Strobe.command: installs it (run this)
  • Read Me.txt: this file


BEFORE YOU START

  • Any Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel) running macOS 14 or later. It was made and tested
    on macOS 27.


INSTALL

  1. Double-click "Install Strobe.command".

     If macOS says it can't be opened ("Apple could not verify…"): click
     Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click
     "Open Anyway", then double-click the installer again.

     Or open Terminal, type  bash  followed by a space, drag
     "Install Strobe.command" into the Terminal window, and press Return.

  2. The installer moves Strobe into Applications and opens it.

  Prefer to do it by hand? Drag Strobe into Applications and open it. The
  first time, macOS blocks it: go to System Settings → Privacy & Security
  and click "Open Anyway".


USING Strobe

  • Start: open a video (or drag one onto Strobe's icon), File → Open from
    Photos (slow motion opens at its full frame rate), or try a sample.
    Strobe reads each frame's own time from the file, so there's no frame
    rate to type in.

  • 1. Scale (S): drag along something you know the length of, such as a
    metre stick, and type its length (1 m, 50 cm, 1.000 ± 0.002 m).
  • 2. Axes (X): click the origin, drag to turn the x axis.
  • 3. Track: Add point (A) — click the object on each frame; clicks snap
    to its centre (hold Option to place a point exactly). Auto-track (T) —
    draw a box round the object; check any hollow points it isn't sure of.
  • Edit (E): drag points, the scale's ends or the origin. Delete removes
    the selected point. Command-Z undoes anything.

  • Everything is a floating panel, the video too: drag one by its title
    bar, resize it from its corner, and show or hide panels in the View
    menu. Auto Arrange (Option-Command-A, or the button beside the panels
    menu) puts each one in a recommended place and keeps them tidy as the
    window changes size, until you move one yourself. Tab hides all the
    panels so the video fills the window.
  • Graph: pick what to plot, drag across it to choose a range, then pick a
    fit. Turn on error bars and max/min gradient lines in its … menu.
  • Give objects a mass for energy and momentum; track two objects for the
    Collision panel.
  • Export: data as CSV, Copy Data (Shift-Command-C) to paste into Google
    Sheets or Excel, graph pictures, a strobe-picture worksheet with an
    answer key, and a movie with the arrows drawn in.

  • Projects are .strobe files with the video inside. Save to Google Drive
    (Option-Command-S) puts them in a Strobe folder in your Drive; Open from
    Google Drive opens them on any Mac or iPhone. Sign in from Settings.
    Strobe only sees the files it saves and the ones you pick.

  • Opening from Photos asks for access to your Photos library.


GOOD TO KNOW

  • To update: Strobe → Check for Updates…, or unzip a newer copy and run
    its installer. It replaces the old one and keeps your settings.

  • To uninstall: quit Strobe and drag it from Applications to the Trash.

  • Strobe is free and open source (MIT), an open-source alternative to
    video-analysis tools such as Vernier Video Analysis and Tracker, and not
    affiliated with either. It isn't from the App Store and isn't notarized
    by Apple, which is why macOS asks you to confirm it the first time.
