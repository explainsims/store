Pigeonhole 1.4.1
Spaces of tabs for Chrome and Edge. Give each project or class its own
space: switch a window to it and its tabs come back just as you left them.
Everything is saved as you browse, and your spaces follow you to your other
browser and your other Mac.


WHAT'S IN THIS FOLDER

  • Pigeonhole.app: the app
  • Install Pigeonhole.command: installs it (run this)
  • Read Me.txt: this file


BEFORE YOU START

  • Any Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel) running macOS 14 or later. It was made and tested
    on macOS 27.
  • Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge (or both).
  • If you use Workona's tab manager, turn it off first (Workona → Settings
    → Tab Management), or remove its extension. Two tab managers moving the
    same tabs would fight.


INSTALL

  1. Double-click "Install Pigeonhole.command".

     If macOS says it can't be opened ("Apple could not verify…"): click
     Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click
     "Open Anyway", then double-click the installer again.

     Or open Terminal, type  bash  followed by a space, drag
     "Install Pigeonhole.command" into the Terminal window, and press Return.

  2. The installer moves Pigeonhole into Applications and opens it. Its
     Settings open at Browsers.

  3. Add Pigeonhole's extension to Chrome (and Edge, if you use it). It's free
     and doesn't need the Web Store:
       a. Click "Open Extensions Page" (or type chrome://extensions in
          Chrome's address bar; in Edge it's edge://extensions).
       b. Turn on "Developer mode" (top right in Chrome, on the left in
          Edge).
       c. Click "Load unpacked" and choose the folder Pigeonhole shows you
          ("Show Folder"). In the folder window, ⌘⇧G and
          ~/Library/Application Support/Pigeonhole/Extension gets you there.
     Settings → Browsers shows "Connected" a moment later.

  Prefer to do it by hand? Drag Pigeonhole into Applications and open it. The
  first time, macOS blocks it: go to System Settings → Privacy & Security
  and click "Open Anyway".


USING Pigeonhole

  • Make a space: click Pigeonhole's button in the browser's toolbar (the
    little cabinet), then "Save window as space". Give it a name and a
    colour. The button's badge always shows which space a window is.

  • Switch: press ⌥A (Option-A) or click the button, pick a space and press
    Return. This window's tabs are swapped for that space's. ⌘-Return opens
    it in a new window instead. Type to search spaces and every saved tab.

  • Everything saves by itself: new tabs, closed tabs, the order, tab
    groups. Close a space (⌥W, or "Close space") and its tabs are kept for
    next time.

  • Pinned tabs (Gmail, say) are in every window: every space, new
    windows too. Pin, unpin or move one and every window follows. If
    Chrome comes back from a restart without them, Pigeonhole puts them
    back. To keep them out of one space, tick "Disable pinned tabs for this
    space" beside its Pinned section on Pigeonhole's page. "Pinned tabs" in
    the page's sidebar lists them, with a history to bring back one you
    closed. In Settings you can make them belong to their space instead.

  • Pigeonhole's page is the first, pinned tab of every window. Its sidebar
    lists all your spaces; click one to see its tabs, double-click to open
    it here. For a closed space, click a tab to open the space at that tab,
    or use the arrow to open just that tab. Opening a space from the page
    keeps you on the page.

  • Tick tabs (the box at the left of each row) to move them, put them in
    a group, close or remove them, or drag them onto a space in the
    sidebar. Each section (Pinned, each group, the other tabs) has its own
    box to tick all its tabs, and an arrow to fold it away. "Group…" puts
    ticked tabs in a new group or an existing one, or takes them out.

  • Drag to rearrange: drag a tab up or down the list (a line shows where
    it lands). Drop it among a group's tabs, or on the group's header, to
    put it in that group; among the other tabs to take it out. Drag a
    group's header to move the whole group. This works for this window's
    tabs and for a closed space's saved tabs. "All spaces" shows each
    space as a card, and / searches every saved tab. Settings → General
    can turn the page off; the switcher's grid button brings it back.

  • Move a tab to another space: ⌥D, or right-click the page → "Move tab to
    space". "New space…" at the bottom makes a new one for it. ⌥N makes a
    new space.

  • The side panel (the panel button in Pigeonhole's popup) lists this
    space's tabs: tick some to move them, put them to sleep or close them.
    "Close duplicates" and "Sort" tidy up.

  • History: the clock button. A snapshot is kept every hour while you use
    a space, and each time it closes. Tick the tabs you want back, or
    "Restore all" to put the space back as it was then.

  • The menu bar: Pigeonhole's icon lists your spaces, how many tabs each has
    and where it's open. Click one to open it in your browser (hold Option
    for the other browser). ⌃⌥A (Control-Option-A) shows the switcher from
    any app. Recently Closed and Archive are there too.

  • Settings (from the menu):
      – General: how switching works. "Quick" keeps the old space's tabs
        ready in one minimised window, so going back is instant (they fall
        asleep after 15 minutes and close after 4 hours). Pigeonhole keeps
        that window minimised, out of your way. "Light" closes
        them and reopens them later. Also: pinned tabs, putting idle tabs
        to sleep, how long history is kept, opening at login, and the
        menu-bar icon's colour.
      – Browsers: setting up the extension, and whether each browser is
        connected.
      – Sync: where your spaces are kept, and your other Mac.
      – Shortcuts: the ⌃⌥A shortcut. The browser shortcuts (⌥A, ⌥D, ⌥N,
        ⌥W) are changed in the browser: chrome://extensions/shortcuts.


YOUR SPACES

  • They're kept in iCloud Drive → Pigeonhole, so your other Mac has them
    too (install Pigeonhole there and add the extension). Nothing is sent
    anywhere else: there's no account and no server.

  • If a space is open on both Macs (or in Chrome and Edge) and changes in
    one, the other asks: "Use theirs" or "Keep this window's".

  • The first time, macOS may ask whether Pigeonhole can use iCloud Drive.
    Click Allow. If iCloud Drive is off, your spaces stay on this Mac.


GOOD TO KNOW

  • To update: unzip a newer copy and run its installer. It replaces the
    old one and keeps your settings and spaces. The extension updates
    itself from the app.

  • To uninstall: quit Pigeonhole and drag it from Applications to the
    Trash. Remove its extension in chrome://extensions. Your spaces stay in
    iCloud Drive → Pigeonhole until you delete that folder.

  • Chrome may remind you now and then that a Developer mode extension is
    installed. That's normal for extensions added with Load unpacked.

  • Pigeonhole is free and open source, an open-source alternative to
    Workona's tab manager (not affiliated with Workona). It isn't from the
    App Store and isn't notarized by Apple, which is why macOS asks you to
    confirm it the first time.
