HD Music 1.8.1
HD Music is a music player for DSD and hi-res audio. It plays DSF, DFF (including
DST-compressed), SACD ISO images, Blu-ray audio, audio CDs and every common PCM format,
and shows you exactly what reaches your DAC. It keeps a library of your music wherever it
is (Mac, external drives, a NAS), and can tidy a whole collection into a new home.


WHAT'S IN THIS FOLDER

  • HD Music.app: the app
  • Install HD Music.command: installs it (run this)
  • Read Me.txt: this file


BEFORE YOU START

  • Any Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel) running macOS 14 or later.


INSTALL

  1. Double-click "Install HD Music.command".

     If macOS says it can't be opened ("Apple could not verify…"): click
     Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click
     "Open Anyway", then double-click the installer again.

     Or open Terminal, type  bash  followed by a space, drag
     "Install HD Music.command" into the Terminal window, and press Return.

  2. The installer copies HD Music into Applications and opens it.

  Prefer to do it by hand? Drag HD Music into Applications and open it. The
  first time, macOS blocks it: go to System Settings → Privacy & Security
  and click "Open Anyway".


USING HD Music

  • Library: Albums, Artists, Songs and Genres at the top of the sidebar
    (⌘1 to ⌘4). Add the folders, drives or network shares that hold your
    music (File ▸ Add Music Folder…, or Settings ▸ Library). HD Music reads
    them once, then only what changes. Albums on a drive that's unplugged
    stay listed, greyed out, until you connect it again. "Connect to
    Server…" finds a NAS or another Mac on your network; macOS asks for the
    password and keeps it in your Keychain.

  • Radio (⌘5): thousands of stations on a world map. Click a place to hear
    what's on there, or search by name, country or genre. Star the ones
    you like. Radio plays through HD Music's own output, so your DAC, EQ
    and Signal Path all apply, and the song title shows when the station
    sends it. Stations come from the free Radio Browser directory.

  • Internet Archive (⌘6): search the Archive's free music, from the
    Live Music Archive's concerts to 78 rpm records and netlabels. Play
    any item straight away: it downloads as it plays, through your DAC,
    EQ and Signal Path. Star items as favourites; Recently Played keeps
    the rest. "Save to Library" keeps a tagged, lossless copy with its
    notes in Music ▸ HD Music Archive (or a folder you choose in
    Settings ▸ Library). If your connection is slow, Settings ▸ Library
    ▸ Internet Archive playback can play the Archive's smaller MP3s
    instead; Automatic picks whichever your downloads keep up with.

  • Remove from Library: right-click an album, artist, song or genre ▸
    Remove from Library… It disappears from the library but its files stay
    exactly where they are. Settings ▸ Library ▸ Removed Items brings any
    of it back.

  • Clean Up (sidebar ▸ Tools): tidies folders where they are. Like
    Organize, it identifies albums (MusicBrainz) and you review them; then
    it fixes the tags, renames the files and sorts them into tidy folders
    inside the folder you chose, and removes folders left empty. Every
    change is recorded first, so "Undo Clean Up" puts everything back. You
    can also have it copy the cleaned-up music to a NAS or another drive
    afterwards.

  • Play Next and Add to Queue: right-click any album or song. The list
    button at the top right shows what's coming up.

  • Get Info (right-click ▸ Get Info…): edit the tags of one track or many
    at once, including the cover (drop an image on it). Changes are saved
    into the files themselves (MP3, FLAC, DSF, DFF, AIFF, WAV, M4A,
    WavPack, APE); only the tags change, never the audio.

  • Organize (sidebar ▸ Tools): tidies a whole collection into a new place,
    such as a NAS or a new drive, leaving the originals untouched.
      1. Sources: the folders and drives your music is on now, and where it
         should go, and how files should be named.
      2. Review: HD Music groups files into albums, fills gaps from file
         and folder names, and identifies albums on MusicBrainz (about two
         seconds an album; it carries on after a quit). Check the ones
         marked "Needs a look"; duplicates in lower quality are left out.
      3. Copy: every file is copied, checked against its original, renamed,
         and given its fixed tags. Covers, booklets and rip logs come along,
         and a report lists everything. Your original files are never
         changed.

  • Mini Player (Window ▸ Mini Player, ⌥⌘M): a small window that stays on
    top. Controls in the menu bar: Window ▸ Controls in Menu Bar.

  • Playlists: drag music files or folders onto a playlist (left sidebar).
    + at the bottom of the sidebar makes a new playlist, or a folder
    playlist that follows a folder on your disk and updates by itself.
    Double-click a track to play it. Right-click for Play Next, Get Info,
    Show in Finder, Convert and Remove. CUE sheets split single-file albums into
    tracks. File ▸ Add Files… (⌘O) works too.

  • Discs: an audio CD in a USB drive appears under Discs with its track
    names and cover (looked up on MusicBrainz; you can turn that off in
    Settings ▸ General). SACD ISO images and Blu-ray folders or ISOs open
    like files. Copy-protected Blu-ray discs can't be played: HD Music
    never breaks copy protection.

  • Output: choose your DAC from the menu at the top right. Settings ▸
    Output (⌘,) has the options for each device. DSD playback: DoP sends
    DSD to the DAC untouched; Convert to PCM is for speakers, headphones
    and AirPods. You can also set exclusive access, the sample rate,
    volume, and how 5.1/7.1 is played (stereo downmix, headphones
    binaural, or one channel per speaker). Exclusive access is off, so
    your Mac's own sound stays on the DAC. Turn it on to keep other
    apps' sound out; macOS then moves its own sound to another output
    while HD Music plays.

  • DSD and volume: DSD reaches the DAC untouched only while the Mac's
    volume for the DAC is at full. Turn it all the way up and use the
    DAC's own knob for pure DSD. If you turn the Mac's volume down,
    HD Music switches to a high-quality PCM conversion so the volume keys
    still work, and goes back to pure DSD at full volume.

  • Adding music: click Music Library at the top of the sidebar, then
    drop folders or music files on it (or on Albums, Artists, Songs or
    Genres), or use Add Folder… and Add Files…. Folders stay where they
    are and are watched for changes. Loose files are copied into your
    Music folder, in "HD Music".

  • Check your DAC: Settings ▸ Output ▸ "Check This DAC…" plays a quiet
    tone at every sample rate, then a DSD tone over DoP at each DSD rate
    and asks what you heard. HD Music then knows exactly what your DAC
    takes. Turn the volume down first.

  • Speakers: Settings ▸ Output has a test tone for each speaker. For a
    5.1/7.1 receiver you can also choose which output each speaker is on.

  • Signal Path (sidebar ▸ Tools, or ⌥⌘1): shows every step from the file
    to your DAC and tells you whether it's Bit-perfect, DSD untouched (DoP),
    or Converted, and why. With a level meter and spectrum.

  • EQ & DSP (⌥⌘3): a parametric EQ with presets (import AutoEQ files for
    your headphones), loudness levelling (ReplayGain), headphone
    crossfeed, balance. Turn it off for bit-perfect playback. To level
    files that have no ReplayGain tags, use a playlist's ⋯ menu ▸ Measure
    Loudness.

  • Converter (⌥⌘2): DSD to FLAC, ALAC or WAV; SACD ISO or DST to plain
    DSF; PCM to another rate or format. Drop files on it, or right-click
    tracks ▸ Convert. CD tracks are read twice and compared, and a
    "Rip log.txt" says whether every track verified.

  • Watchdog: if another app changes your DAC's sample rate while you
    listen, HD Music tells you and puts it back.

  • Keys: Space plays and pauses, ⌘← and ⌘→ skip, ⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→ jump
    10 seconds. Media keys and Now Playing work too.

  • Online: HD Music goes online only to look up audio CDs, to identify
    albums and fetch covers in Organize (MusicBrainz and the Cover Art
    Archive), for Radio (the Radio Browser directory and the stations
    themselves), and for the Internet Archive section (archive.org). The first time you connect to a server, macOS asks whether
    HD Music may look for devices on your local network.


GOOD TO KNOW

  • For a Sony PHA-3 or another USB DAC: set DSD playback to DoP. DSD64
    and DSD128 then reach the DAC untouched. Higher rates are converted to
    the best PCM rate the DAC takes.

  • Dolby Atmos and DTS:X play their full lossless 7.1 bed. Rendering the
    height objects needs Dolby's and DTS's own software, which isn't
    available to open-source players.

  • To update: unzip a newer copy and run its installer. It replaces the
    old one and keeps your settings and playlists.

  • To uninstall: quit HD Music and drag it from Applications to the Trash.
    Your playlists and library catalogue are in
    ~/Library/Application Support/HD Music.

  • HD Music is free and open source (MIT). It includes FFmpeg, libbluray
    and dsd-nexus under the LGPL; their licences and source are inside the
    app (Contents/Resources/Licenses). It is an open-source alternative to
    Sony's Hi-Res Audio Player and is not affiliated with Sony. It isn't
    from the App Store and isn't notarized by Apple, which is why macOS
    asks you to confirm it the first time.
